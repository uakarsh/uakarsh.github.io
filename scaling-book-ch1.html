How to Scale Your Model, Chapter 1: rooflines

Akarsh Upadhyay  ·  October 2026

What this book should let you do:

  • Ballpark how close parts of your model are to their theoretical optimum.
  • Make informed choices about different parallelism schemes at different scales.
  • Estimate the cost and time required to train a large Transformer model.
  • Design algorithms that take advantage of specific hardware affordances.
  • Design hardware driven by an explicit understanding of what limits current algorithm performance.

Questions to answer:

  • How long should a matrix multiply of a certain size take? At what point is it compute-bound vs. memory-bound?
  • How are TPUs wired together to form training clusters? How much bandwidth does each part of the system have?
  • How long does it take to gather, scatter, or re-distribute arrays across multiple TPUs?
  • How do we efficiently multiply matrices that are distributed differently across devices?

Chapter 1 pointers (rooflines):

  • Three limits. Compute (FLOPs/s), bandwidth (bytes/s) and memory capacity. Runtime is bounded below by max(Tmath, Tcomms) when the two overlap, and above by their sum.
  • Arithmetic intensity is FLOPs per byte moved, a property of the algorithm. You are compute-bound only when it exceeds the hardware's peak FLOPs/s divided by bandwidth.
  • The roofline is achievable FLOPs/s = min(peak, AI × bandwidth). Taking the reciprocal flips the max into a min, which I got wrong on my first pass.
  • Matmul has AI ≈ B (batch size) when B ≪ D, F. That makes the threshold about 240 on a TPU v5e and about 298 on an H100 (3.35 TB/s HBM, dense bf16).
  • Weight precision matters. With int8 weights and bf16 activations the threshold halves to about 120, because each weight costs one byte instead of two.
  • Reuse is where intensity comes from. A dot product has AI ≈ ½, and a matmul with a different weight matrix per batch element is stuck at AI = 2, about 0.4% of int8 peak. Decode attention over per-sequence KV caches is the same story.
  • Network rooflines count only the bytes crossing the link. I had mixed HBM traffic into the network term; each chip has its own HBM roofline separately.

These are my notes on How to Scale Your Model. The full notes include the worked exercises, a roofline plot across several D and F, and my corrections: typeset notes (PDF) and handwritten notes (PDF). More chapters in the repo as I get to them.
